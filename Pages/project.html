Tython Temple

Tython Temple is my longest project that I have worked on. It took me 2 years to complete, albeit on and off, while working. It was more of a passion project by the time I was nearing the end, unlike a lot of my other projects that I worked on; this one was almost entirely made by me. There were only 3 rooms of the entire project that were tampered with by other people. The building consisted of: The medbay, casino, library, main lobby, exterior, hangar, star map room, classrooms, a secret vault, and the garden. The images above are (from top left to bottom right) the casino, hangar, medbay, and exterior. The exterior had a larger space for players to explore, as well as an entrance to the interior. The trees and some of the terrain are attributed to other members of the team, but I made the rest. I added swimming physics, day and night lighting, ambience audio, and the entrance to the building, which had to unload the exterior and load the interior. The casino took the longest, with interactive cups, a secret backroom for VIPs, and a large enclosed moss environment with lights and particles to add a mystical flare to the place. The medbay was the last place that I made for the temple, with four surgery bays (what I chose in the image to represent the medbay) with interactable tools, four recovery rooms, a central station with tubes and wiring to add to the mystical feeling of the temple, and a waiting room with a functional call machine.

Laser Guns

Laser Guns is a spanning project; I didn't include every single model inside of my examples above, but I did include some of my favorites. There were at least three variants of the minigun (top right) where I made different models, tested different firing speeds, and even projectile types. The lasers themself are presets that I took as an asset from a Rec Studio, but it came very customizable for me to be able to change colors, brightness, and intensity. The minigun was simpler than the other weapons, as it didn’t need any other mechanics besides projectiles, lights, sounds, and a spinning barrel. The Shotgun was my most complex one. I made it with a side light to shine on targets, a bloom shot, pump action with scenarios for both racked and unracked, as well as a chamber that ejected a large shell when needing a new clip. Not a typical shotgun, since it uses a “clip,” but everything is fair in space laser weapons. The snipe had a functional scope, which I couldn’t show at the time, since it was mainly meant for VR players to use, and it used a clip reloading system as well, and had a front grip to help angle better. The pistol (bottom left) was one of my first laser weapons, so I decided to include it. I made it about 2 years ago when I was just starting my journey.

Underscant

Underscant is one of my older projects that I worked on; my skills were nowhere near as refined as they are today, but I included it because it is one of the biggest programming-related projects that I had to work on. I could go on about all of the buildings, and how it took 2 weeks with 5 hours a day to make it, but I could also tell you about the 2 months of working on programming to create a functioning economy for the map. This project was supposed to be a game; needless to say, it was forgotten pretty quickly after release, and you can only find it on Rec Room by the name of “Underground Coruscant” by the name of the guy I sold it to for $100 named “ImArtemis”. The game itself was about living as an underground member of society, violence is a regular occurrence, and people sell ‘spice’, a starwars inspired drug, and collect bounties for money. The game stored everybody's information on servers paid for by Rec Room, but also saved their karma. Karma resulted in people gaining bounty and becoming bigger targets for other people. It was largely a PvP game, but after a few hackers got in, the devs stopped focusing on it as much, so it faded away. I was given ownership of the game before I sold it, since the person who did most of the programming didn’t want the game. I took it as a second choice. Still one of my favorite projects, but also really depressing looking back on it.

POU:R

Work in progress project that I started roughly 2 months ago as of February 2026. A friend of mine has made a game called “Protocol of Undead: Revamped” or “POU:R”, which I had been watching development for a bit on, and asked if I could give a shot at making maps using Roblox Studio, which I had only done a few mock attempts at before. This would be my first project doing so, and it is still work in progress as I’ve been spending a lot of time on school work rather than my projects. The map consists of a “red team, blue team” map, although in POU:R it is a zombie survival game, and not PvP. I plan to later include an upper section of the map; you can probably see some progress done in the top left image, and I plan to include more “ammo rooms” to give the players a better fighting chance. The game is out right now on Roblox if you’re interested in checking it out, made by Snake_Studio by a guy called snakecool1. The difference between Rec Studio, Blender, Unity, and Roblox Studio is almost non-existent; everything is extremely similar, so I feel like this will be a simple and fun experience for me.
